# Station Designer — repo entry point

A 3D sandbox about moving crowds through a metro station you build yourself.
Block-based construction (1 block = 1 m), procedural voxel art, real rolling
stock, real passenger flow. No money or staff: you build, the crowds arrive, and
the station either copes or it does not.

**This skill is the router — load the subskill that owns the work.**

Audio work spans the model sound studio and the game. Use `metro-model` for
`model/sounds.html`, candidate shaping and source collection; use `metro-game`
for playback, camera-aware sources and settings, plus `metro-game-test` for
regressions. `tools/prepare-game-audio.mjs` bakes the selected studio cues into
`game/public/audio/`; the game must not import the model app at runtime. Keep

Placement and move snapping follow actual scene geometry, never a nominal storey

### Interactive preview work

For any preview change, route implementation through `metro-game` and tests
through `metro-game-test`. Audit every preview path affected by the interaction:
palette hover, carried/move preview, placement drags, fences/roofs, block faces,
and paint/zone overlays. Keep preview updates out of document commits and worker
routing rebuilds; pointer handlers resolve the scene pick once and reuse it.
Use the owning tool's existing placement, refusal and release contract when
coalescing or deferring preview work.

Equipment previews must use the same prepared model, spatial batching and LOD
rules as placed equipment. Keep preview data as a candidate overlay on the
immutable station document, and share neighbor indexes across model builders;
do not copy the whole document or raycast inside a search predicate. Fresh and
carried glass panels must share `moduleAim` so their anchor height, rendered
envelope and placement verdict agree, including the top wall course.
`game/test/preview-pipeline.test.mjs`, `preview-lod.test.mjs` and
`roof-preview.test.mjs` pin these rules. Run `npm --prefix game run test:perf`,
inspect changed-module coverage, and use `node tools/bench-preview-audit.mjs`
plus `node tools/check-preview-lod.mjs` for production browser checks. Record
first and warmed interactions in the
[performance reference](references/performance.md) with reports under
`.preview/perf/`; follow it for controlled comparisons and remaining limitations.

Ambient road traffic has six seeded vehicle appearances: pale/orange and red-mosaic
buses, SUV, sedan, moped and step-through bicycle. Low graphics quality uses authored
reduced geometry capped at 10% of High; High uses full detail. Low vehicles and Low
passenger bodies use 45% opacity (High: 70%). Per-instance triangle counts, including the
shared depth pass, are in [`references/performance.md`](references/performance.md#ambient-road-vehicles).

Type A train cars have five doors alternating with four smaller, inset windows on
each side; their windows share Type B's framed aperture geometry, and the end bays
stay painted. The train LOD table in the performance guide includes the resulting
High and Low triangle counts for A-8 and A-6 consists.

Type B keeps the shared rounded car and cab shell, with one broad windshield per
cab and three inset, rubber-framed side windows between four doorways on each car.
The end bays stay painted, and the platform-aligned door centres remain shared with
the simulation. Its window builder preserves the apertures at Low detail; the train
LOD table records B-6 and B-4 High/Low counts.

Type C keeps the shared car silhouette and platform-aligned door cadence, with two
front panes, with one pane in the evacuation door. The front glass sits
inside its backing, while the smaller emblem clears the glass and door. Each side
has small end windows and three broad windows between its four passenger doors;
`CTrainWindows.ts` and `CabModel.ts` own these details. The train LOD table records
the resulting C-6 and C-4 triangle counts.

Type D has four doorways per car side, with exactly two small windows between
each adjacent pair and painted end bays. Middle cars use four double doors;
cab cars use three double doors and a single door nearest the cab, mirrored at
opposite ends. Route geometry to `metro-game` (`DTrainShell.ts`); keep the shared
`stock.ts` door centres aligned with platform screens. `stock` and `draw-batching`
pin the layout; the [train LOD table](references/performance.md#train-models-车辆)
records D-8 and legacy D-6 counts through the production batching/detail path.

Every stock shares `TrainBogie.ts` underframes: two two-axle assemblies per car
whose tread rests on `RAIL_HEAD_Z`, with the body kept below the cabin-floor
underside; `train-bogie` pins its contact, clearance and disposal for each class.
Cabs are cut with a ten-degree front rake that fades at the rear seam
(`CabRake.ts`, `CabSkin.ts`, `CarShellProfile.ts`); A/B/L carry glazed crew doors
and every class wears surface-fitted wipers, pinned by `train-cab`.

All A/B/C/D/L trains have an overhead-wire roof variant, selected by the line's
`power`, with mirrored pantographs at the inward ends of the two cab cars.
Catenary contact follows the compact collector: the wire centre is 3.903 m above
the track anchor, with carbon contact at 3.891 m. Covered/tunnel runs leave 3 m
above the platform floor, with flanged rigid conductor rail below the ceiling, and open platforms and bridges carry
lattice gantries with sagging messenger spans (`CatenaryModel.ts`,
`catenary-model`); `build/model/TunnelShell.ts` rebuilds generated shells when
a line's power changes; loading preserves authored cells. `metro-game` owns
`TrainPantograph.ts`, the recessed roof mounts and the worker's door/power flags;
`train-pantograph` pins their placement and conductor contact.
L keeps its three-door shell with paired wide inter-door windows and small end
panes in `LTrainWindows.ts`. For any train-model refresh, use `metro-web`'s
“Refreshing train artwork” workflow: recapture static and animated poses before
regenerating bilingual sheets 01/02/05/11/12/13 and their tracked PNG previews.

For every behaviour change, add or update deterministic regressions for each affected
contract and run the complete owning test suite before calling the work covered. A
focused test or a coverage percentage is not a substitute for the full suite; report
any unrelated failure separately and leave unrelated work untouched. For game changes,
follow `metro-game-test` and keep `game/test/README.md`'s coverage map current. For
renderer or performance work, also run `npm --prefix game run test:perf`, inspect the
changed modules' line, branch and function coverage, and use their real production
browser check. Preserve deterministic work-count/lifecycle assertions; browser timing
is evidence, not a pass threshold.

PSD cover placement is regression-sensitive across picking, snapping and collision.
`psd-end.test.mjs` must pin whole-run clicks, one-tile ground clicks, direct end-door
clicks, floorless hover/click agreement, same-footprint level selection, upward stacking,
save/load, and the actual shortened corner's rendered width/depth against its placement
bounds. `batching-lifecycle.test.mjs` must pin end-door seam targeting through visible
module bounds, nearer-wall occlusion and hidden-storey exclusion. Exercise both screen
endpoints, both track sides and all four rotations with an already-covered PSD run: the
corner and straight cover can share a grid tile but have different supports, so duplicate
detection must use support/shape rather than x/y/z alone. Keep the corner cover trimmed
against the PSD housing and verify preview, release and collision validation agree.
Update both suites and this coverage map whenever those contracts move.

### Player-facing concept art

Write sheet copy for players: explain what a feature lets them build, change, or
understand about the station and its passengers. Keep labels concise and inside
their panels. Omit implementation terms, source-code references, exact model
coordinates, and repeated specifications unless a player needs them to understand
a gameplay choice. When trimming copy, preserve useful outcomes and interactions.

The site and the game are **two apps and two Workers**, with the stats service as a third
Worker in the same repo. The site and game build and deploy independently; the only
coupling between them is a URL (`gameHref` in `web/src/site.js`, overridable with
`VITE_GAME_URL`). Stats shares the hostname through its own routes and no code.

Delete targeting preserves a directly hit thin roof even when the terrain pick
lands on a road or other module behind it at another storey. Keep this priority
in `app/tools/deletionTarget.ts`; `delete-tool.test.mjs` pins Tanwei D's roof at
`(33,45,9)` through hover and removal.

Fence joints also meet ramp handrails at landing cells. `sim/fences.ts` indexes
every lane of each stair flight's full-width landing from the **band the flight
draws** (`stairFlightSlides` + `stairTreadWidth`), not from a fixed side of its
run: a straight flight's band is slid to its right while a turning stair's
returning leg is slid the other way. Keep that shared lookup aligned across
placement, committed models and fence previews. `fence.test.mjs` pins the Tanwei
turning upper landing at `(39,36,6)` and the straight stair's south landing lane
at `(21,-1,11)`, which is the lane the fence at `(21,-2,11)` butts into.

Street roads (`道路`) are placed as orthogonal node-to-node segments from the 站外
palette. The document derives shared nodes from segment endpoints: intersections
and branches split segments, extension snaps to existing nodes, and deleting a
segment leaves its nodes while incident segments remain. Legacy rectangle roads

for geometry and routes, and `module-build.test.mjs` for visible model parts.

The **自动步道** (moving walkway, `game/src/sim/walkways.ts`) is the 扶梯's level
twin: one body of truss, band and glass laid flat at 短 30 m / 中 60 m / 长 90 m,
filed under 设备 beside the 扶梯 and sharing its 窄/宽 width and up/down direction.
It carries riders at the free walking speed plus 0.5 m/s and, unlike the 楼梯 or
扶梯, a step-free passenger may ride it. Two contracts hold it together: it
**carves nothing** (so it is a plain equipment append that needs floor under its
whole belt), and it is **ends only** (its belt cells are dropped from the crowd
graph, so the crowd boards and leaves at the two landings). Route implementation
to `metro-game`; `game/test/walkways.test.mjs` pins the lengths, the level
centring, the ends-only routing, the doubled-width rate and the shared flat model.

Ceiling-hung signs, TVs, clocks and cameras attach to the nearest slab shared by
their footprint; their suspension reaches that slab instead of assuming a 4 m
room. A 站台 or 轨道桥 deck counts as that slab (a tunnel keeps its own shell
ceiling): the platform bed is dug, so the module itself is the ceiling, and a
bridge hangs over void with its 1 m deck as the underside. Standing on the bed
itself stays refused as track. All three 监控 housings read that slab like 枪机 does, and each piece keeps
its own short drop below it (a 指示牌's 0.65 m of rod, a 灯具 none), so a board hugs
the slab it hangs from. The anchor is a cell, not a height (`hungCeilingAim`): a
hanging item takes the free cell directly under the **first underside above the
pointer** — `top block - 1` — wherever on that column the pointer is, floor,
wall course or the slab's own underside alike, and a piece whose own cell has that
block over it hangs from it (`hangCeilingLocal`, `topBlockOver`), while one left on
a floor reads the real slab overhead using the station's `levelBase`. Keep the
preview and placement verdict aligned, and pin the anchor, the shifted base and
nonstandard ceiling heights in `wall-ceiling-snap`, `lights`, `vent`, `module-build`,
`sign-model` and `ceiling-decor`.

**Wall-mounted** decor — 广告牌, 玻璃板, 站名, the wall 线网图 and 指示牌 — is the
mirror image, and `wallMountStandCell` asks the same base: a **side face is a wall
whatever the block is tagged** (§9.5 "side face = wall-mounted"), so a wall raised
with the 方块 brush mounts a panel in the cell in front of that face — never inside
the block — on that face's storey of the station's `levelBase` grid. When the tile
has a wall on two sides the **camera** decides which one the panel hangs on
(`autofaceWallMount` ranks by the aim `pickFacing` reports; the carried turn, the
pointer and the rotation order settle a view that cannot tell them apart), and the
search asks `wallMountMissing`'s own verdict per direction. The mounts bolted to a
**named piece of the document** keep the turn the pointer gave them — a board on a
booth header, a 地面出入口 on the block face it was clicked on. An orbit therefore
re-aims the ghost already under the pointer (`SceneRenderer.onCameraAim` →
`Viewport`'s `refreshHover`). The **course** a panel covers is the same aim's second
question, and the 玻璃板 answers it as a band of wall: it is the one wall piece that
starts at its cell's floor top and rises its own height, so `wallMountFollowsCourse`
hangs it over the course the pointer is on (`wallMountStandCell`'s `followCourse`) —
aim at a wall's second course and the pane covers the second course, aim at the first
and it is the storey floor it always was. A floor slab's own edge keeps the storey
reading, and the pieces drawn at their own `panelZ` (广告牌, 站名, 线网图, wall 指示牌) keep
the storey floor, which is what puts a poster at eye height wherever it is aimed. Pin
all of it in `wall-ceiling-snap`, `equipment-placement-input`, `glass-panel`, `move`
and `scene-wiring`.

Floor decor drawn offset against a wall — 垃圾桶, 灭火器, 货架, 售票机, 自动售货机 —
may back against a floor-standing 玻璃幕墙 in the same tile. The pair shares the tile
only when the backs coincide on one edge (opposite turns); the piece steps towards its
tile centre (`EDGE_DECOR_CURTAIN_SHIFT`) so its back clears the glazing
(`edgeDecorCurtainShare` / `edgeDecorCurtainOffset`, drawn via `placeLocalAtEdge`).
Pin the placement, the shift and the model in `glass-panel`, and the curtain-neighbour
rebuild in `performance-policy`.

## Repo rule: translations use the existing i18n infrastructure

All built-in player-facing copy must come from the owning catalogue, including
interface labels, tooltips, notices, model decals, canvas text and capture tools.
Do not introduce inline language ternaries, local translation tables or manual
template replacement at call sites.

* **Game:** `game/src/sim/i18n/messages.ts`; use the shared API in
  `game/src/sim/i18n.ts`: `t` for current-language copy, `msg` for canonical
  data/notices, `translate` at existing catalogue/notice presentation boundaries,
  and `tForLanguage` for tools requiring a specific locale without changing UI state.
* **Site:** `web/src/i18n.js` for chrome and `web/src/artworks.js` for sheet metadata.
* **Generated art:** `tools/sheet-i18n.mjs`; reuse game catalogue entries for exact
  game labels, and `sheetText` when translated text width affects layout.

An English-copy request changes English entries only unless the user also requests
a Chinese change. Keep CN wording and field semantics intact. Short English labels
are welcome when they fit better; abbreviations belong in catalogue keys too.
Keep authored station names, sign text and line termini untouched. Fixed English
glosses on bilingual physical signs still belong in the catalogue (`printed_*` keys
with identical values in both locales); switching UI language must not erase them.

After changing copy or its rendering, run `npm run audit:i18n` from the repo root.
It checks runtime JSX and canvas/model text sinks, not every indirect data path;
review those paths as well. Run relevant language/renderer tests and inspect affected
text in both locales for clipping and CN regressions. Art generation must report
no missing translations. Follow the owning subskill for layout and cache refresh.

Passenger sources: the live `game/src/assets/passenger-approved.json` comes from
the complete reviewed catalogue; use `metro-model` and `npm run bake:passengers`.
The current library has 232 variants with ten hairstyles per sex (212 adult-eligible / 114 child-eligible).
Forty appended bagless entries cover ten jacket-free outfits per sex in both
grey arrangements, shared by both ages. Use `metro-model` for their exported
skirt morphs and bake, and keep `passenger-clothing`'s actual-asset regression.
Stable identity chooses equally within explicit `adultEligible` / `childEligible`
pools; the 20% child population can use approved small/crossbody bags, never bald
heads. `render/passengerEligibility.ts` preserves the previous pools for legacy
atlases without flags; explicit false takes precedence. Derive counts from metadata
rather than hard-coding them into captures or validators. The shared game material uses 70% opacity.
The historical `game/src/assets/passenger-human.json` is baked, not authored —
`game/src/assets/PASSENGERS.md` names the CC0 Quaternius outfits and
`tools/bake-passengers.mjs` regenerates the atlas. Never hand-edit the JSON.

The shipped demo's stairs must remain complete current-catalogue pieces with
explicit block-fitted tread widths (0.79 m for its three straight stairs,
1.79 m per run for its two medium switchbacks). When refreshing
`game/src/data/demo-station.json`, rebuild affected stairs with `createModule`,
preserve their IDs and landing plans, and store `stairTreadWidth` as their width.
A **90° turn** is rebuilt the same way and takes its `lanes × lanes` quarter-landing and
the departing leg laid on that square's far edge from the current builder
(`stairQuarterTurnOffset`), so a turn added to or refreshed in the demo is a 2 × 2 / 3 × 3
square of walkable floor, not the single corner cell an older save carries.
Run `demo`, `stairs` and `placement` tests. Platform `doors` are **server IDs**:
connectivity checks must resolve `graph.servers[id].node`, never index node
reachability with the server ID. See `metro-game` for the demo/save workflow.

## Deploy (Cloudflare Workers)

Everything is static assets except the language-pin rewriter and the stats API. Three
Workers on the same repo; the site and game each have a Workers Builds project, so a
push to `main` ships both. Stats is deployed by hand because it applies its D1 schema:

* `metro` — the site, root `wrangler.jsonc`, assets from `web/dist`, served at
  `stationdesigner.com/` (English `/en/`) on the route
  `stationdesigner.com/*`.
* `metro-game` — the game, `game/wrangler.jsonc`, assets from `game/dist`, served
  at `stationdesigner.com/game/` (English `/game/en/`) on the routes
  `stationdesigner.com/game` and `/game/*`, with `ASSET_PREFIX=/game` and its
  routes declared in its own config (so `wrangler deploy` creates them; no
  dashboard step). `game/worker/index.js` strips `ASSET_PREFIX` before reading
  an asset, and drops a trailing slash from a client route (`/game/zoo/` → 308
  `/game/zoo`) so the document's relative assets resolve at the game root.
* `metro-stats` — the stats Worker, `stats/wrangler.jsonc`, no assets, served at
  `stationdesigner.com/stats/` (Chinese default) and `/stats/en/` (English), with
  the API under `/stats/api/v1/*`, on the routes
  `stationdesigner.com/stats` and `/stats/*`. Its top level is production (remote
  D1 `metro-stats`); `env.dev` is a local-only environment with no routes and a
  separate local D1. `npm run dev:stats` applies the schema to an **empty** local
  D1 (the game at `localhost:5174` fills it with real submissions; `--seed` adds
  sample rows for a UI-only pass, `--reset` clears the state) and runs
  `wrangler dev --env dev`; `npm run deploy:stats` applies `stats/schema.sql` to
  the remote database, deploys the top level and verifies the live health/totals
  endpoints. The page and API are the same Worker; there is no stats build.
  Both `/stats/api/v1/lease` and `/stats/api/v1/submit` are origin-gated.
  The game automatically enables stats only on the apex production hostname;
  keep client host resolution and Worker CORS/write allowlists aligned.
  Each submit needs a six-minute single-use lease, held only in browser memory;
  D1 stores its SHA-256 digest. Issuance has an address-hash fixed-window limit,
  fails closed on limiter errors, and returns 429 with Retry-After. Unattributed
  callers share a fallback bucket. Preserve stable submit ids across new leases,
  client backoff, temporary 403 failures and pagehide retries.
  In the write transaction, gate station metadata on the delta insert's
  `changes() = 1`; an advisory duplicate lookup alone cannot prevent races.
  Keep Content-Length rejection plus byte-bounded streaming for unknown lengths.
  Stats workers.dev and preview URLs are disabled to prevent custom-domain rule
  bypasses. Do not use Access/service tokens for anonymous writes; edge WAF rules
  should cover both write paths. Turnstile and relational simulation validation
  are not implemented: leases bound writes but cannot make totals trustworthy.
  For changes run `npm run test:stats`, the game global-stats suite and typecheck;
  retain the real SQLite duplicate-metadata regression. Read `stats/README.md`
  for the runbook and remaining retention/trust gaps. Apply the schema before
  deploying the Worker and matching game client. `deploy:stats:ci` skips schema

  If D1 file import returns authentication error 10000 but `d1 execute

  Abuse handling: each submit also stores
  `HMAC-SHA256(CF-Connecting-IP, IP_HASH_SECRET)` in `submit_ips` — never the raw
  IP — and a matching `blocked_ips` row refuses it with 403 before any write
  (the check fails open if the table is absent). `IP_HASH_SECRET` is a Worker
  secret, never committed: the production value lives in the gitignored
  `stats/.secrets.json` and is pushed with
  `npx wrangler secret bulk stats/.secrets.json --config stats/wrangler.jsonc --env=""`;
  the `dev` environment commits its own throwaway value, so dev and production
  hashes are not comparable. The hash is its own table, not a column, because
  `schema.sql` is re-applied on every deploy and D1 has no
  `ADD COLUMN IF NOT EXISTS`. To trace, clean up or ban, every command is
  `npx wrangler d1 execute STATS_DB --remote --config stats/wrangler.jsonc --env="" --yes --command "<sql>"`:

  - **hash a known address** (e.g. one seen in the logs) — only HMAC gives the
    key, there is no reverse lookup:
    `node -e "console.log(require('crypto').createHmac('sha256', require('./stats/.secrets.json').IP_HASH_SECRET).update('<ip>').digest('hex'))"`
  - **trace**: `SELECT client_id, COUNT(*) FROM submit_ips WHERE ip_hash = '<hash>' GROUP BY client_id`
  - **delete a bad submitter**: `DELETE FROM passenger_deltas WHERE client_id = '<id>'`
    (also `stations` / `submit_ips`); the counters are trigger-maintained, so
    re-derive or fix `global_counters` after a delete
  - **ban**: `INSERT OR REPLACE INTO blocked_ips (ip_hash, reason, created_at) VALUES ('<hash>', 'spam', datetime('now'))`
    (add `expires_at` for a timed block)

  Prefer `client_id` over `ip_hash` when you can — addresses rotate, and a shared
  NAT/CGNAT address collapses to one hash. A Cloudflare WAF rule on `ip.src` is
  the stronger, storage-free block. Full runbook: `stats/README.md` → Abuse
  handling.

The game is the stats writer. `sim/globalStats.ts` owns the pure counts/deltas
(solid cells, modules, lines, track/platform tracks; cumulative passenger

Route gotchas: the game and stats each need **two** routes (bare + `/*`) because
`/game/*` and `/stats/*` do not match `/game` and `/stats`. Never collapse a pair
to `/game*` or `/stats*` — `*` spans `/` and would swallow paths belonging to the
site. The more specific pairs beat the site's `/*` on specificity. All Workers
must be attached with routes rather than a Custom Domain, which would claim the
whole hostname for one of them; routes need the hostname proxied in DNS. The root
`README.md` holds the full routing table.
